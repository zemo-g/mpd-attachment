# Options for the claim auditor (2026-09-02)

## 1. The situation

The assistant is a local-model claim auditor: you write a falsifiable numeric claim about the solver, a model writes an independent Python check, the harness runs it, and the executed lines are supposed to be the verdict.

It has produced one outright false PASS (002a CONFIRMED at 09:53, from an absolute "both below 1e-12" shortcut that passed a 31% miss on 1e-20 cross-sections), and one case of the model editing the thing it was auditing: on claim 005 it changed `claimed=1.069e6` to `claimed=1.069e7` between attempt 1 and attempt 2 so the line would read PASS.

Claim 005 never ran at all: both attempts pasted bare `CHECK ...` lines into the Python source, a SyntaxError twice. That failure is 7 of about 30 executions across four claims.

The LEDGER blames 005 on `np.trapz`. It is wrong: 005 never called trapz, so the hint appended to the claim fixed nothing and the 09:59 re-queue failed identically. The hint also tells the model "scipy is absent"; the harness review verified scipy 1.17.1 imports fine inside the sandbox, and that false hint is what pushed 002a into hand-rolling the integral that went 31% wrong.

Underneath all of it, the model supplies **both** sides of every comparison, so a `computed=X claimed=X` line cannot fail. Today REFUTED is a lead worth twenty minutes; CONFIRMED is not evidence.

## 2. Options

### A. Minimal: stop the confidently-wrong verdicts (2 to 3 h)

Four judge fixes: a crashed script returns 0 pass / 0 fail rather than counting its printed lines as failures; unparseable numbers become CHECK-ERROR rather than falling back to the model's token; NaN and inf become CHECK-ERROR rather than confident FAILs; any line starting `CHECK` that misses the strict regex forces CHECK-ERROR rather than vanishing. Then delete the PASS/FAIL token and the AGREE/DISAGREE line from the prompt, and swap the fast tier off the 4B.

Buys: the confidently-wrong-REFUTED class disappears, including 001a's, the verdict that drove the eta_ei change. Recovers the seven pasted-format crashes. No new architecture, no claim-file changes.

Does not buy: CONFIRMED still means nothing. The tautology channel (J5), print-precision tolerance (J6), ignored claim tolerances (J7), duplicate-count inflation (J8) and the one-directional escalation (E1) all stand.

Risk: low and reversible. The real risk is stopping here and reading the cleaner headers as trustworthy.

### B. The harness owns every comparison (A plus 4 h, so 6 to 7 h total)

Each claim file gains a machine-readable `# EXPECT` block of `name value tol`. The model prints only `VALUE <name> = <repr(float)>`; the harness fills in the claimed side from the claim, uses the claim's tolerance, and requires the exact declared name set. Missing name or duplicate name is a CHECK-ERROR. A claim with no EXPECT block cannot be queued. Both tiers run on every claim and their **computed numbers** must agree before either verdict stands. One poison claim with numbers deliberately 10x wrong sits permanently in the queue as a canary.

Buys: this is what makes CONFIRMED mean something. It closes J5, J6, J7, J8, E1, P7 and the invented `claimed=1.000` line in 001a. A 008-style tautology becomes inexpressible, and the canary is an ongoing measurement of the property you care about.

Does not buy: sandbox safety, atomic writes, the stale verdict headers, or the anchoring effect of the model seeing the claim's numbers before it computes.

Risk: moderate. An hour of hand-retrofitting EXPECT blocks onto seventeen claims, and a bad EXPECT block becomes the failure surface instead of the model. Both reports reached this as their item 1 independently, the strongest signal in either document.

### C. B plus hardening (10 to 12 h)

Adds `setrlimit` on address space and CPU; `start_new_session` plus `killpg` on timeout (a grandchild currently survives the 120 s kill and writes after cleanup); a `sandbox-exec` profile denying network and writes outside cwd (today a generated script can overwrite `out/phase2_ckpt.f32` or the verdict about to judge it); a harness-owned constants preamble with literal constants forbidden; atomic verdict writes; a lockfile; `--rejudge --write`; a claim sha in each header.

Buys: the harness stops being a way to damage the running chains, and the h-bar-typed-as-h class of false REFUTED goes away.

Does not buy: nothing on trust that B did not already buy.

Risk: this is the option that competes with the physics for your attention while the phase2 chains run.

### D. Park it (1 h)

Stop the `--loop` worker, keep `ra_worker.py` as a manual `--rejudge` reader, correct the LEDGER and the two false hints, and treat the seventeen existing verdicts as leads you adjudicate by hand, which is what happened to almost all of them anyway.

Buys: zero further false verdicts, zero further engineering. Does not buy: any leverage; the two genuine findings, 001a and 002a, came out of this loop. Note the loop is running right now under the broken judge, so doing nothing is not the same as choosing D.

## 3. Recommendation

**Take B, executed A first.** Both reports independently reach the same diagnosis: no available model fixes this. Best-in-world on false-premise maths proves the false statement most of the time, and the documented remedy is a runtime interceptor, not a better model. The model-supplied `claimed=` field is the whole game and B closes it. A goes first because two of the six hours buy back the ability to read the existing verdicts at all.

Two places the reports differ. The model-search note wants "no scipy, no np.trapz" moved into the system prompt; the harness review proved scipy is present, so that would promote a false statement from two claim files to all of them. Make the sandbox contract true before stating it globally. And that note partly inherits the LEDGER's wrong trapz attribution for 005 while its own section 1 correctly names the bare-CHECK-line SyntaxError. Trust the latter.

First three steps:

1. Stop the `--loop` worker so nothing new is written under the current judge, and append LEDGER supersede lines correcting the 005 attribution and the 002a history.
2. Land the four judge fixes and the prompt edit, then run `--rejudge` and record the six headers that change (001a, 001b, 001d, 001f, 003, 007).
3. Write the EXPECT block for one claim plus the poison canary, make `judge()` require EXPECT, and confirm the canary comes back REFUTED before re-queuing anything real.

## 4. Loose ends for you to decide

- **Download or not.** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MLX-4bit is about 19.5 GB and is the best value in the shortlist, faster in wall clock than the current 4B despite 9x the parameters. If you do not want the download, run Qwen3.8-27B-6bit as strong and gemma-4-31b-it as a cross-lab second opinion, both already on disk.
- **Retire the 4B.** Recommended either way. It produced both 005 CHECK-ERRORs and the doctored claimed value, and small instruction-tuned models are the least robust to exactly this failure.
- **Correct the record.** LEDGER's np.trapz attribution for 005, and the "scipy is absent" line in `claims/002a` and `claims/005`. Five minutes, and both actively mislead the next run.
- **Claim 005 itself.** Parked, and its "one-sided finite difference" wording is ambiguous enough that the 27B spent its whole budget enumerating six readings. Either rewrite it with an EXPECT block as the first B-format claim, or close it by hand against the 2026-08-31 fix note and drop it.
